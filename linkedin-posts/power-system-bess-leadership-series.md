## Post 1: Grid-forming BESS is the new spinning reserve

Coal plants gave us something we never paid for.

Inertia.

Every retired synchronous machine removes a little of the stability the grid used to get for free.

Grid-following inverters can't replace it, because they follow the grid. Grid-forming BESS can, because it sets voltage and frequency itself.

That changes the question for boards:
→ Not "how many MWh do we buy?"
→ But "how much system strength do we own?"

Three things I'd ask any storage team today:
1. Is the PCS grid-forming capable, or only grid-following?
2. Has the OEM model been validated in EMT studies, not just RMS?
3. Is there a revenue route for inertia or system strength in our market yet?

Storage that only does arbitrage is a trading asset.
Storage that does grid-forming is infrastructure.

Which one is your portfolio building?

## Post 2: The warranty clause that quietly kills BESS IRR

Most BESS business cases look great in year 1.

The trouble starts in year 7.

Here's what I see repeatedly in LTSA and warranty reviews:
• Capacity guarantee tied to a cycle count your dispatch strategy will exceed
• Augmentation "optional" in the model, but mandatory in reality
• SOH measured by the OEM's BMS, not an independent test
• Rest-SOC and temperature limits that ops can't realistically hold

None of these show up on the first slide. All of them show up in your IRR.

My checklist before signing:
✔ Model degradation using the actual dispatch profile, not a generic 1 cycle/day
✔ Price the augmentation schedule upfront
✔ Insist on a witnessed annual capacity test
✔ Align the warranty cycle definition with the PPA/tender obligation

Cheap cells don't give you a cheap project. A well-written contract does.

## Post 3: Storage is becoming a transmission asset

Building a new transmission line can take 5 to 10 years.

A BESS can be energised in 12 to 18 months.

That's why grid operators are starting to treat storage as a *virtual transmission line*:
• Charge upstream of congestion
• Discharge downstream at peak
• Defer or right-size the line

Europe is already doing this with grid-booster projects. The same logic applies to every renewable-rich corridor in India and the Gulf where curtailment keeps rising.

For utilities and TSOs the shift is subtle but big:
storage stops being a generation asset and becomes a network planning tool.

The regulatory question is the hard part. Who owns it, who dispatches it, and how is it remunerated without distorting markets?

Get that framework right and you unlock more GW of renewables than any single line will.

## Post 4: Solar is cheap at noon. The grid's real problem is 7 PM.

Solar won the daytime.

The evening peak is where the money and the risk now are.

Every GW of new solar steepens the evening ramp, and that's why tenders are moving fast from:
→ Plain solar
→ Solar + 2-hour storage
→ FDRE / round-the-clock / peak-assured supply

For developers, this changes what "winning a bid" means:
• Firmness and peak availability now beat the lowest tariff
• Storage sizing becomes a core bid strategy, not an add-on
• Penalty clauses for shortfall decide margins

Leaders who still think of BESS as a "solar accessory" will be outbid by teams who think in delivered firm MW.

What's your organisation's storage ratio on new bids today?

## Post 5: Fire safety is now a board-level BESS risk

One BESS fire can change a market's permitting regime overnight.

We've seen it happen. Large-scale incidents have triggered moratoriums, local opposition, and much tighter insurance terms.

For directors, safety is no longer just "the EPC's job." It's licence-to-operate risk.

What I expect to see in every board pack for a storage project:
1. Large-scale fire test data (UL 9540A or equivalent) at the unit level, not just the cell
2. Compliance basis: NFPA 855 / IEC 62933 / local fire code
3. Container vs indoor architecture, and the reasoning behind it
4. Off-gas detection, deflagration venting, and the emergency response plan agreed with the local fire service
5. Thermal runaway propagation test results

Investors are asking these questions now. Better that your team answers them before they do.

#BESSSafety #EnergyStorage #RiskManagement #NFPA855 #BoardGovernance

## Post 6: Battery prices fell. Who actually captured the value?

LFP cell prices have dropped sharply over the last two years.

But many project costs haven't fallen nearly as much.

Where did the savings go?
• PCS, transformers, and MV switchgear: long lead times, firm prices
• Grid connection and land
• EPC risk premiums
• Augmentation and LTSA reserves

The lesson for leadership: the battery is no longer the main cost lever. **Balance of system, interconnection, and execution are.**

The teams winning tenders now are optimising:
→ DC block standardisation
→ Higher-voltage DC architecture
→ Fewer PCS/MVT skids per MW
→ Faster commissioning and testing

Cell price is a market input. BoS is a management decision.

#BESS #LFP #EnergyStorage #EPC #CostOptimization

## Post 7: The BMS already knows which rack will fail. Is anyone looking?

A 500 MWh plant produces millions of data points a day: cell voltages, temperatures, SOC, insulation resistance.

Most of it is never analysed.

Yet the early warnings are usually there:
• Cell voltage spread slowly widening in one rack
• A thermal hotspot that repeats on every discharge
• An HVAC unit losing efficiency weeks before it trips

Analytics and digital twins turn that data into:
✔ Predictive maintenance instead of reactive callouts
✔ Independent SOH tracking (the leverage you need in warranty claims)
✔ Higher availability, which is where your revenue sits

For asset owners the ROI case is simple. A single avoided outage or a single won warranty claim usually pays for the analytics layer.

Storage assets are software-defined now. O&M teams need to be too.

#DigitalTwin #BESS #AssetManagement #PredictiveMaintenance #AIinEnergy

## Post 8: 4-hour storage won't be enough

The industry standardised on 2 to 4 hour lithium storage.

The next decade's problem is different: multi-day low-wind/low-sun periods, seasonal shifts, and 24×7 clean power for heavy industry.

That's where long-duration storage comes in:
• Pumped hydro (still the largest by far)
• Flow batteries
• Compressed/liquid air
• Thermal storage
• Green hydrogen for seasonal balancing

Lithium will remain dominant for short duration. But portfolio thinking matters:
→ Short duration = arbitrage, ancillary services, peak
→ Long duration = adequacy, firmness, resilience

Leaders planning a 2035 generation mix with only 4-hour batteries are planning for the average day, not the worst one.

What duration is your organisation planning for?

#LongDurationStorage #LDES #PumpedHydro #EnergySecurity #NetZero
