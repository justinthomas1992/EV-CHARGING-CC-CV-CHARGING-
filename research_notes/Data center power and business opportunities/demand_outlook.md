Research notes compiled 2026-10-28. Labels used: **[OFFICIAL]** = government or intergovernmental body (IEA, DOE/LBNL, European Commission, CSO Ireland, PJM, ERCOT, MeitY or state governments); **[INDUSTRY]** = brokers, consultancies, research institutes (EPRI, JLL, Cushman & Wakefield, Wood Mackenzie, Rystad); **[COMPANY]** = corporate guidance or announcements; **[SECONDARY]** = aggregators or trade blogs, use with caution. Session note: www.iea.org could not be fetched directly (network allow-list), so IEA figures come from search-result extracts of IEA pages and PDFs, plus secondary coverage.

## Q1. Current and projected data center electricity consumption (TWh) and capacity (GW): global, US, EU, China, India, GCC

### Takeaway
Official central estimates for global data center use are about 415 TWh in 2024 (1.5% of world electricity) and 485 TWh in 2025. They are projected to roughly double to about 945-950 TWh by 2030 (around 3%). The US and China account for about 80% of the growth. US official ranges are much wider: LBNL gives 325-580 TWh by 2028 and EPRI's 2026 update gives 9-17% of US electricity by 2030. The EU is smaller (68 TWh in 2024, rising to 114 TWh in 2030 per the Commission). India and the GCC are small in TWh terms but growing fastest in percentage terms.

### Inferences
- Implied global average load: 485 TWh / 8,760 h is about 55 GW of average draw in 2025, and 950 TWh is about 108 GW by 2030. Installed IT/critical capacity is higher once utilisation and PUE are taken into account.
- The US official range (LBNL's 325-580 TWh by 2028) brackets the IEA trajectory. The EPRI 2026 high case (17% of US electricity, roughly 700+ TWh assuming about 4,200-4,400 TWh of total US use) would on its own approach 75% of the IEA's 2030 global figure. That suggests the IEA central case may be conservative if US announcements materialise.
- China projections disagree by about 3x (277 to 774 TWh), so any China-based business case needs scenario bands.

## Q2. AI-specific load: rack densities, cluster sizes, gigawatt campuses, hyperscaler capex 2025-2026

### Takeaway
AI racks have gone from about 10-15 kW (legacy) to about 120-155 kW (GB200 NVL72). NVIDIA's Kyber/Rubin Ultra platform targets about 600 kW per rack in 2027. Flagship campuses are now planned at 1-5 GW: Meta Hyperion is 5 GW, Stargate Abilene about 1.2 GW, and xAI Colossus 2 targets about 2 GW. Big-4 hyperscaler 2026 capex guidance is about $720-745B, versus about $410B in 2025.

[INDUSTRY/COMPANY]
- Rubin Ultra NVL576 "Kyber" racks: about 600 kW per rack (confirmed by Jensen Huang), shipping in H2 2027. — [Introl](https://introl.com/blog/nvidia-vera-rubin-gpu-600kw-racks-2027); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/analysis/nvidia-gtc-jensen-huang-data-center-rack-density/)

  - Combined: about $720-745B; the FT tally is about $725B, up 77% from about $410B in 2025.

### Inferences
- A 5x rise in rack density (about 120 kW to about 600 kW) within roughly 3 years makes liquid cooling, 800 VDC distribution and on-site generation or storage the default for new AI halls. Power, not floor space, is the binding siting constraint.
- About $725B of 2026 capex corresponds, at an industry rule-of-thumb of very roughly $10-15B per GW all-in for AI capacity including chips (an inference, not a sourced figure), to tens of GW per year of new AI capacity in the pipeline.

## Q3. India: policy, installed and pipeline MW, CEA/Grid-India projections, IndiaAI compute

### Takeaway
India has about 1.8 GW operational (C&W H1 2026) and a 3.9 GW pipeline. Mumbai holds about half of it. The national data centre policy is still a draft (September 2025: 20-year tax holiday, four Data Centre Economic Zones). States are the active policy makers; Uttar Pradesh targets 2 GW. The IndiaAI Mission has about 38,000 GPUs onboarded.

### Cited Findings

Chennai about 214 MW operating with a 368 MW pipeline. — [Safeguard India AI DC map](https://safeguard.sh/resources/blog/india-ai-data-centre-map-mumbai-chennai-hyderabad-visakhapatnam). JLL says Mumbai and Chennai lead hyperscale deliveries. — [JLL](https://www.jll.com/en-in/insights/market-dynamics/india-data-centers)

### Inferences
- 38,000 GPUs at about 1-1.5 kW each all-in implies only about 40-60 MW of IT load. The IndiaAI public compute pool is small next to the 1.8 GW commercial base. Commercial hyperscale and colocation demand from Mumbai and Chennai drives grid load.
- If the 13.56 GW FY32 planning figure is right, India would need roughly 7x today's operating base within about 6 years. That has large implications for state discom tariffs, green open access and transmission in Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Telangana and UP.

### Inferences
- The GCC's announced AI pipeline (UAE 5 GW, HUMAIN 6+ GW, DataVolt 1.5 GW, Oman 0.4 GW) is more than 10x current operating capacity (about 0.9 GW for the UAE and KSA combined). Delivery risk is high: chip export licences, the redesign of the UAE campus, and NEOM scaling back.
- At full build-out, 5 GW running at a high load factor would draw about 35-40 TWh/yr. That is a large fraction of current UAE electricity generation, which is not sourced here and should be checked. Dedicated gas, nuclear (Barakah) and solar-plus-storage supply is therefore implied.

## Q5. Share of national electricity demand and grid-planning implications

### Takeaway
Data centres already take 23% of metered electricity in Ireland and about 25% in Virginia. US grid operators now forecast data centres to drive most or all near-term peak growth. PJM forecasts about 222 GW peak in 2036. ERCOT forecasts +50 GW of peak over 5 years, about 30 GW of it from data centres, with large-load queues of several hundred GW. Ireland replaced its de facto Dublin moratorium in December 2025 with a "bring your own power" connection policy.

### Cited Findings
- **US national share [OFFICIAL]:** 4.4% in 2023, rising to 6.7-12% by 2028 (LBNL). [INDUSTRY] EPRI puts it at 4-5% today, rising to 9-17% by 2030. — [Berkeley Lab](https://newscenter.lbl.gov/2025/01/15/berkeley-lab-report-evaluates-increase-in-electricity-demand-from-data-centers/); [EPRI](https://esca.epri.com/products/3002034696/)
- **US states [INDUSTRY]:** Virginia is at about 25% today and could reach 39-57% by 2030 (EPRI). By 2030, data centres could exceed 20% of electricity in Arizona, Indiana, Iowa, Nebraska, Nevada, Oregon and Wyoming. — [Energy Digital/EPRI](https://energydigital.com/globenewswire/3245491)
- **PJM [OFFICIAL]:** 2026 Load Forecast (14 Jan 2026) projects summer peak growth of 3.6%/yr to about 222,000 MW in 2036 and 253,077 MW in 2046. Large loads (mostly data centres) account for more than 100% of 5-year peak growth as base demand contracts. A 50% haircut is applied to non-firm projects. All-time peak was about 168,158 MW on 2 Jul 2026. — [Modo Energy](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/pjm-load-forecast-data-centers-2046); [Yes Energy](https://www.yesenergy.com/blog/demand-projections-when-where-how-much); [Noreva](https://noreva.ai/pjm-load-forecast-2026-data-center-demand-capacity-energy-prices/)
- PJM capacity market stress: reports of a $325/MW-day price cap and FERC action. — [Enki (secondary)](https://enkiai.com/policy-and-regulations/pjm-capacity-market-data-centers/)
- **ERCOT [OFFICIAL via secondary]:** adjusted forecast of +50 GW of peak in 5 years, about 30 GW from data centres. New peak of 91.1 GW on 22 Jul 2026. Large-load queue figures conflict:
  - 410 GW as of 26 Mar 2026, 87% data centres.
  - 198 GW applied in Q1 2026 alone.
  - About 226 GW, about 77% data centres (DC Hub).
  - Sources: [Ascend Analytics](https://www.ascendanalytics.com/blog/large-load-interconnection-queues-data-center-grid-access); [GridVision](https://gridvisionai.com/interconnection-queue/large-loads); [DC Hub](https://dchub.cloud/interconnection-queue)
- **Ireland [OFFICIAL]:** CSO 2025: data centres used 7,663 GWh (+10% y/y), 23% of metered electricity, up from 22% in 2024, 21% in 2023 and 5% in 2015. EirGrid's median scenario reaches 31% by 2034. — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/data-centers-power-use-emea-ireland/); [Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/m-irelands-data-centres-used-5-per-cent-of-the-countrys-metered-electricity-in-2015-but-by-2025-they-were-using-23-per-cent-more-than-every-urban-household-in-the-country-put-together/)
- **Ireland policy [OFFICIAL]:** EirGrid's de facto block on new Dublin-area connections (from 2021) ended in December 2025 when the CRU's Large Energy User connection policy took effect. New data centres must match maximum import capacity with on-site or nearby generation or storage. Sites of 1 MVA or more must source at least 80% of annual demand from additional Irish renewables, on a 6-year glide path. — [Silicon Republic](https://siliconrepublic.com/?p=1109690); [CRU202504 proposed decision](https://consult.cru.ie/en/system/files/materials/200/CRU202504%20LEU%20connection%20policy%20proposed%20decision_0.pdf); [IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/ireland-unveils-strict-new-rules-for-data-centre-power-use)
- **Singapore [OFFICIAL]:** Green Data Centre Roadmap (May 2024) adds at least 300 MW in the near term, plus 200 MW or more if green-powered. The 2019-2022 moratorium ended in 2022, and the 2023 pilot awarded only 80 MW across 4 proposals. More than 90% of power comes from natural gas. — [EDB Singapore](https://edb.gov.sg/en/business-insights/insights/singapore-to-expand-data-centre-capacity-by-at-least-one-third-pushes-for-green-energy-use.html); [Capacity Media](https://capacitymedia.com/article/2davfx8ylwhh1au8329kw/news/singapore-to-free-up-300mw-for-data-centres)
- **EU [OFFICIAL]:** data centres reach 3.2% of EU electricity demand by 2030 (COM(2026) 500). — [EC report PDF](https://build-up.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2026-10/39-2026-48_Report%20on%20the%20energy%20efficiency%20of%20data%20centres%20in%20the%20EU.pdf)
- **China [INDUSTRY]:** about 2.3% of national demand by 2030 (Rystad); 5-6% in the highest estimate. — [Oilprice/Rystad](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/Chinas-Data-Center-Boom-Could-Double-Power-Demand-by-2030.html); [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/explainer-how-china-is-managing-the-rising-energy-demand-from-data-centres)

### Inferences
- Policy is converging on "bring your own power or flexibility" (Ireland's matching rule, Singapore's green conditions, US large-load tariffs and queue reforms). This favours on-site generation, BESS and demand-flexibility offerings.
- Queue figures in the hundreds of GW (ERCOT) far exceed realistic build. Utilities apply haircuts (PJM 50%), so announced demand should be discounted heavily in any market sizing.

- No Grid-India or state-discom large-load forecasts were found for India.
- No GCC utility load forecasts were found.
- ERCOT queue figures were not verified against primary ERCOT filings.
